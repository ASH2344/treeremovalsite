#### What Is Stump Grinding?

Stump grinding uses a machine with a spinning cutting wheel to chip a stump down below ground level. The stump turns into wood chips, which can be used as mulch or raked out and replaced with topsoil. The roots stay underground and break down naturally over time.

#### How Does a Stump Grinder Work?

The grinder's cutting wheel has hardened teeth that spin at high speed. The operator moves the wheel side to side across the stump, lowering it a few inches with each pass until the stump is ground below the surface. Smaller machines fit through backyard gates, so most stumps can be reached.

#### Stump Grinding vs. Stump Removal

|  | Stump grinding | Full stump removal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happens | Stump ground below ground level | Stump and main roots dug out |
| Yard damage | Minimal | Large hole to fill |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Most yards, lawns, gardens | Building sites, where roots must go |

Most homeowners choose grinding. It's faster, cheaper, and easier on the yard.

- **Pest control.** Rotting stumps attract termites, carpenter ants, and fungus.
- **Stop regrowth.** Some trees send up new shoots from the stump and roots.
- **Use your space.** Plant grass, a garden bed, or build over the spot.

**Signs Your Tree Is Diseased (and When to Remove It)**

A diseased tree doesn't always look sick at first. By the time large limbs start dropping, the decay may be well advanced. This guide shows the warning signs to look for in Middle Georgia yards, the most common problems, and when a tree can be saved versus when it should come down.

#### 7 Warning Signs of a Diseased Tree

1. **Peeling, cracked, or missing bark.** Bark falling off in patches, or deep cracks in the trunk.
2. **Mushrooms or fungus at the base or on the trunk.** Often a sign of internal or root rot.
3. **Dead branches in the canopy.** Bare limbs while the rest of the tree has leaves.
4. **Discolored or spotted leaves out of season.** Brown, yellow, or spotted leaves in spring or summer.
5. **Oozing sap or dark wet patches** on the bark.
6. **Hollow sounds or soft wood** when you tap the trunk.
7. **Sudden leaning or lifted soil** around the roots.

#### Diseased Tree Bark: What to Look For

Healthy bark is firm and attached. Watch for bark that sloughs off in large pieces, sunken or cracked areas called cankers, holes from boring insects, or sawdust-like material at the base. On pines, look for small pitch tubes (blobs of resin) on the trunk, which often point to bark beetles.

#### Common Tree Problems in Middle Georgia

| Problem | Trees often affected | Signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Root and butt rot | Water oaks, laurel oaks | Mushrooms at base, hollow trunk, leaning |
| Pine bark beetles | Loblolly and other pines | Pitch tubes, browning needles from top down |
| Hypoxylon canker | Oaks under stress | Bark peeling to show gray or tan crust |
| Branch blight and dieback | Many species | Dead tips and branches spreading inward |
| Leaf spot and anthracnose | Oaks, dogwoods, sycamores | Spotted or brown leaves, early leaf drop |

This is a general guide. A professional inspection is the best way to confirm what's affecting your tree.

#### Treating Diseased Trees

Some problems can be managed when caught early:

- **Prune out infected branches** to slow the spread of blight and dieback.
- **Reduce stress** with watering during drought and avoiding root damage.
- **Treat insect problems early**, before they spread to nearby trees.

Treatment rarely works once rot reaches the trunk or major roots.

**When to Trim Pine Trees in Georgia**

The best time to trim pine trees in Georgia is **late winter to early spring**, before new growth starts. Pruning at the wrong time can stress the tree and attract pests, especially pine bark beetles in Middle Georgia's hot summers.

#### Best Time to Prune Pine Trees

| Season | Good time to trim pines? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late winter to early spring | Best | Tree is dormant, beetles less active, wounds heal as growth starts |
| Late spring | OK for light pruning | New growth ('candles') can be shaped |
| Summer | Avoid heavy pruning | Fresh cuts release scent that attracts pine beetles |
| Fall | Light work only | Wounds heal slowly heading into winter |
| Any season | Yes, for dead or dangerous limbs | Safety comes first |

#### Why Summer Pruning Is Risky for Pines

When a pine is cut, it releases resin with a strong scent. In warm months, that scent can draw pine bark beetles, which bore into the tree and can kill it. Middle Georgia's long, hot summers make this risk higher than in cooler regions.

#### When Should I Trim My Other Trees?

For most hardwoods in Macon, like oaks, maples, and pecans, **late fall through winter** is the best time. Leaves are off, so branches are easy to see, and the tree is dormant. Spring-flowering trees like dogwoods are best trimmed right after they bloom.

#### How to Prune Pine Trees the Right Way

- **Remove dead and broken limbs first.** These can be cut any time.
- **Raise low limbs gradually.** Don't remove more than a quarter of the live canopy at once.
- **Cut just outside the branch collar.** The swollen ring where the branch meets the trunk heals best.
- **Never top a pine.** Cutting off the top ruins the tree's shape and invites decay.
- **Leave tall pines to professionals.** Climbing and cutting at height is dangerous.
