How Much Does Tree Removal Cost in Macon, GA?

Most tree removals in Macon cost roughly $250 to $1,000. Small trees can cost less, while large, hazardous, or emergency removals can cost well over $1,000. This guide breaks down what affects the price so you know what to expect.

Pine Tree Removal Cost

Pines are the most common removal in Middle Georgia. Tall loblolly pines are straight and easier to section than spreading hardwoods, but their height adds cost. A typical pine removal near a house often falls in the medium-to-large range above.

Oak Tree Removal Cost

Oaks have heavy, wide-spreading limbs and dense wood, so they often take longer to remove than pines of the same height. Water oaks with hidden rot may need extra care, which can raise the price.

Stump Grinding Cost

Stump grinding is usually priced separately. A single average stump often costs roughly $100 to $300, depending on its width and access. Multiple stumps in one visit usually lower the cost per stump. See stump grinding

Emergency Tree Removal Cost

Emergency and storm jobs typically cost more than scheduled work, often 1.5 to 2 times as much, because of urgent scheduling, after-hours work, and the extra care needed around damaged homes. See emergency tree removal

What Affects Tree Removal Cost?

Factor Why it matters
Height and trunk width More cutting, rigging, and time
Location Trees near houses or lines need sectional lowering
Condition Dead or rotten trees can be unsafe to climb
Access Backyards with narrow gates limit equipment
Cleanup Hauling wood and debris adds cost
Permits Some Macon removals may need one

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Tree Removal?

Often yes, when a tree falls on a covered structure like your house, garage, or fence. Insurers usually won't pay to remove a healthy tree as prevention, or a tree that fell without hitting anything. Check your policy and take photos before work starts.

How to Save on Tree Removal

  • Bundle several trees or add stump grinding in one visit
  • Keep the firewood instead of paying for haul-off
  • Schedule non-urgent work in late fall or winter
  • Remove risky trees before a storm turns them into emergencies
