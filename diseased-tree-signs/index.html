Signs Your Tree Is Diseased (and When to Remove It)

A diseased tree doesn't always look sick at first. By the time large limbs start dropping, the decay may be well advanced. This guide shows the warning signs to look for in Middle Georgia yards, the most common problems, and when a tree can be saved versus when it should come down.

7 Warning Signs of a Diseased Tree

  1. Peeling, cracked, or missing bark. Bark falling off in patches, or deep cracks in the trunk.
  2. Mushrooms or fungus at the base or on the trunk. Often a sign of internal or root rot.
  3. Dead branches in the canopy. Bare limbs while the rest of the tree has leaves.
  4. Discolored or spotted leaves out of season. Brown, yellow, or spotted leaves in spring or summer.
  5. Oozing sap or dark wet patches on the bark.
  6. Hollow sounds or soft wood when you tap the trunk.
  7. Sudden leaning or lifted soil around the roots.

Diseased Tree Bark: What to Look For

Healthy bark is firm and attached. Watch for bark that sloughs off in large pieces, sunken or cracked areas called cankers, holes from boring insects, or sawdust-like material at the base. On pines, look for small pitch tubes (blobs of resin) on the trunk, which often point to bark beetles.

Common Tree Problems in Middle Georgia

Problem Trees often affected Signs
Root and butt rot Water oaks, laurel oaks Mushrooms at base, hollow trunk, leaning
Pine bark beetles Loblolly and other pines Pitch tubes, browning needles from top down
Hypoxylon canker Oaks under stress Bark peeling to show gray or tan crust
Branch blight and dieback Many species Dead tips and branches spreading inward
Leaf spot and anthracnose Oaks, dogwoods, sycamores Spotted or brown leaves, early leaf drop

This is a general guide. A professional inspection is the best way to confirm what's affecting your tree.

Treating Diseased Trees

Some problems can be managed when caught early:

  • Prune out infected branches to slow the spread of blight and dieback.
  • Reduce stress with watering during drought and avoiding root damage.
  • Treat insect problems early, before they spread to nearby trees.

Treatment rarely works once rot reaches the trunk or major roots.

When Should a Diseased Tree Be Removed?

Removal is usually the safer choice when:

  • More than half the canopy is dead
  • The trunk is hollow, cracked, or badly decayed
  • There's fungus at the base, signaling root rot
  • Pine beetles have spread through the tree
  • The tree leans toward your house, driveway, or play area
